WFS/CALL-SHEET/01
What the day actually looks like

Nine blocks. Seven hours. One film at the end of it.

We start at noon because a person with a job has a Saturday, not a magic hour. Every block below is a real duration, not a promise.

12:00 – 13:45

Learn

Introductions, then the top twenty per cent of what actually matters: story, framing, sound, and the three shots every scene needs. Teams handed over. Script and assignments out.

13:45 – 14:15

Lunch

On us, and you eat it with your captain. This is not a break. It is where the film gets argued about.

14:15 – 16:15

Shoot

Two hours of making it. Phones on Saturday, laptops on Sunday. Everything within two hundred metres. Nobody sits in traffic.

16:15 – 17:15

Cut

Edit, sound, music, titles with everyone's name in them. There is a hard export, and somebody always asks for ten more minutes.

17:15 – 19:00

Screen

Everyone watches everything. Applause after every single film. Feedback, photographs, and at least one person filming the screen.

The rule that makes it work

Everything is shot within two hundred metres of the venue. The cafe, the terrace, the stairwell, the street outside. It sounds like a limitation. It is the only reason your film gets finished, and it forces a real scene instead of a montage of establishing shots.

Questions people actually ask

Before you decide.

I have never made anything. Is this for me?

Yes, and that is the whole point. The weekend is built for people with zero experience. If you have made films before you will still learn things, but you are not the person we designed it for.

Do I need a camera or any equipment?

No. Saturday is shot on phones and Sunday is made on laptops. If someone on your team owns a tripod or a clip mic they can bring it, but nothing is required. Bring a charger.

Do I need to bring a team?

No. Most people come alone and we place them in a team of five. If you want to arrive with your own five, you can do that too.

Is lunch included?

Yes. Lunch is on us, and you eat it with your captain. That half hour is where most teams work out what their film actually is.

Who teaches?

Someone who does this for a living. A resource person teaches the room, and a captain stays with your team from noon until the screening.

Where do the actors come from?

The Nobody Club directory, which is free for actors to list on. You shortlist, and we help you make the calls on the day. Team members are also allowed to act, and often do.

Do I have to travel around the city to shoot?

No, and this is deliberate. Everything is shot within two hundred metres of the venue. It sounds limiting until you realise it is the only reason your film gets finished.

What if my film is bad?

It will be. So was everyone's, including the founder's, which is currently in a folder marked do not open. The point is that it exists, you finished it, and you now know exactly what you would fix. That last part is the whole education.

Can I do the AI day without technical skills?

Yes. The AI day teaches the tools from zero. What matters more is story sense, and that is taught too.

Who owns the film?

You do. Your film stays yours and your credits stay yours. We hold a permanent non-exclusive right to screen and promote it, which is how it ends up at our screenings and on our channel.

Do you get my film into festivals?

We do not submit on your behalf and we promise no selections. What we do is share the festival calendar, tell you which ones actually fit your film, and help you get your submission materials in order. The films are yours to send.

Is there a refund?

Full refund up to seven days before the weekend. After that the venue and the resource person are already paid, so we move you to the next batch instead.
